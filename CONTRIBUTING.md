# Contributing

Thank you for considering a contribution. This list covers **US insurance regulatory compliance and cybersecurity**: the NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law (MDL-668), NAIC ORSA and Risk-Based Capital, the McCarran-Ferguson Act and state market conduct examinations, and NAIC accreditation and model-law adoption tracking.

## What belongs here

- Official NAIC model laws, guidance manuals, and handbooks, and their state-adoption tracking resources.
- Statutory text and official guidance directly tied to the state-based insurance regulatory system (McCarran-Ferguson, individual state insurance codes where they implement a covered model law).
- Implementation guidance: risk-assessment methodologies, examination handbooks, model-law adoption trackers.
- Commercial GRC and risk-management platforms with a mature, named feature set for insurance carriers or insurance-industry risk management.
- Certification and training paths specific to insurance regulatory compliance and market conduct examination practice.
- Government and standards bodies that own or maintain the frameworks above (NAIC, IAIS, state departments of insurance).

## What does not belong here

- In-depth coverage of GLBA or NYDFS 500. Both apply to insurers and may be *mentioned* here, but full coverage lives in the [finserv-compliance](https://github.com/garynair/finserv-compliance) companion list — submit substantive GLBA/NYDFS 500 entries there instead.
- NIST CSF, ISO/IEC 27001, and PCI-DSS content, which belongs in the [Security Frameworks](https://github.com/garynair/security-frameworks) companion list.
- COBIT, COSO, and SOX/ITGC content, which belongs in the [IT Audit & Controls](https://github.com/garynair/it-audit-controls) companion list.
- Individual state department of insurance pages submitted one at a time. Use or improve the NAIC's own state insurance department directory entry instead of adding all 56 jurisdictions separately.
- Generic insurance industry or insurtech content with no regulatory or compliance angle.
- Vendor marketing content without a substantive free tier, open-source component, or named feature.
- Self-promotion of products you have not used or do not work with.

## How to submit

1. Open an issue titled `[Submission]: Resource Name`, or open a PR directly.
2. Add the entry in alphabetical order within the relevant section, in the format: `- [Name](URL) - one sentence on what it is, one sentence on why a practitioner would care.`
3. No marketing language, no unverified performance claims.
4. If you are the author or maintainer of the resource, disclose that in the issue or PR description.

## Style

- British English in the description copy.
- No emojis.
- Avoid banned vocabulary (delve, leverage, harness, robust, seamless, holistic, transformative, paradigm). Plain language wins.
- Note a model law's state-adoption status where relevant rather than implying it is universally in force — insurance regulation is state-by-state, and an entry that reads as nationally binding when it is not is a factual error, not a style nitpick.

## Quality bar

- Must be publicly accessible (official government/NAIC source, free tier, or open-source), or a mature commercial platform with a named feature set.
- Must be actively maintained (commit or update within 12 months, or a standards/regulatory body with an active update cycle).
- Description must be factual, and every URL must resolve to the resource it claims to describe. Do not submit a link you have not verified.

## Review

A maintainer reviews PRs within seven days. Most PRs that meet the criteria above land within two weeks.
